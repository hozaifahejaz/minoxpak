Minoxidil 5% or 2%: which strength is right for you?

Both strengths contain the same medicine. The difference is how much of it reaches your scalp, and that changes how well it works and how likely side effects are.

Updated 12 November 2026

For men: 5% works better

In a 48 week trial of 393 men, 5% minoxidil regrew about 45% more hair than 2%, and men using 5% saw results sooner (Olsen et al., 2002). Itching and scalp irritation were a little more common with 5%, but most men tolerated it well. That is why 5% is the standard strength for male pattern hair loss.

For women: 2% is the usual starting point

2% twice a day is the strength most often recommended for women. Higher strengths can work too, but they are more likely to cause fine unwanted hair on the forehead, cheeks or upper lip. This usually goes away after stopping, but many women prefer to avoid it. If 2% is not enough after six months, a dermatologist may suggest a different plan.

Things that matter more than strength

  • Using it every day. Missing doses is the main reason minoxidil seems not to work.
  • Giving it time. Judge it after 4 to 6 months, not 4 weeks. Early shedding is normal.
  • Putting it on the scalp, not the hair. Part your hair and spray directly onto the thinning area.
  • Keeping going. Minoxidil works only while you use it. If you stop, the extra hair slowly sheds.

Minoxidil is not for under 18s, or during pregnancy or breastfeeding. If you have a heart condition or take blood pressure medicine, ask your doctor first.

Common questions

Can a man use 2% minoxidil?

Yes, but it usually works less well than 5%. Some men with a sensitive scalp start on 2% and move up.

Can a woman use 5% minoxidil?

Some women do, under a doctor’s advice. It can work better but is more likely to cause unwanted facial hair.

Does using more than 1 mL work faster?

No. More than 1 mL twice a day does not speed up results and raises the chance of side effects.
